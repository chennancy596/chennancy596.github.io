Moon & Bunny

See You on the Moon

Yuanyuan, Daisy, and a story folded into a paper rabbit.

A story in memories

A Little Home in Beijing

0Prologue

Paper Rabbits

In a game I love, a girl named River spends her life folding paper rabbits. They fill the house like snow that never melts. Everything she cannot say, she folds into them.

I am a girl who loves rabbits too. So I have folded a story into one, and left it here.

IChapter One

The First Meeting

The day we met, he read me a poem. I did not know yet that some poems are written for later. I told him I was a bunny. He laughed, and did not argue.

His name was Will. He walked too fast, and I was always half-running to keep up. I teased him about his music. Later, I knew every one of those songs by heart.

IIChapter Two

A Little Home in Beijing

It was raining the day I arrived in Beijing, and the road was all mud. We carried my things home one by one, shoes soaked through, and neither of us complained. Later the sky cleared, and we lay side by side, watching the clouds come apart.

It was a very small apartment. He took me to the gym and watched me grow a little stronger each time. He brewed my herbal medicine and reminded me to rinse my nose. We ate together, argued about films late into the night, and wandered through shopping malls with nowhere to be. I would sit on the edge of the bed, waiting for the door to open.

“In those ordinary days, that tiny apartment really felt like home.”

IIIChapter Three

Yuanyuan and Daisy

There were two daughters in that home. The soft white one is Yuanyuan. The one in the graduation cap is Daisy.

Yuanyuan had a sharp tongue and loved to complain about her father behind his back. Every time, he solemnly declared that he loved both daughters exactly the same. At night they slept under one blanket, head against head.

Daisy's cap is a wish: that one day her mother will wear a master's cap of her own.

IVChapter Four

That Long Summer

Then came a long summer. Two young people loved with everything they had, and neither yet quite knew how. The right words always came too late, and what each of them meant stayed just out of reach, somewhere behind the fog.

The rain went on for a long, long time.

In the end, we did not reach the clear sky together.

VChapter Five

See You on the Moon

In that game, a boy tells a girl that they will meet on the moon anyway. I have always loved that line. People who lose each other still truly met.

His last gift to me was a record I had wanted for a long time. I keep it with Yuanyuan.

John

“Then we'll meet on the moon anyway, silly!”

∞Epilogue

This Home

That little home was real. The rainy days, the walks, the late-night arguments, two rabbits under one blanket: all of it was real. The ending does not make any of it untrue.

Yuanyuan lives with me now, and Daisy lives with Will. The sisters live apart, one keeping me company and one keeping him company, and between them they remember that little home. I still mean to see the heavy snow I imagined as a child.

If we ever meet again in some corner of the world, I think we will both be okay.

Dr. Rosalene

“The ending is never more important than the journey.”
